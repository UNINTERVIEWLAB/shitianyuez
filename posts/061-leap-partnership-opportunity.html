A promising partner is not yet a funded workstream

Partnership Development & Resource Mobilization

2026.11.22

Capability, JD map and objective

Partnership Development & Resource Mobilization · Sprint Day 5 · 22 November 2026 · 10–15 minutes.

JD connection: identify partnership opportunities, support donor engagement, concept notes and proposals, and coordinate contributions to the work programme. Objective: turn a relevant contact into a bounded opportunity with complementary roles, resources, safeguards and a clear next decision. Read for 5 minutes; draft privately for 5–10 minutes.

Start with a delivery gap, not a partner list

Partner mapping asks which capability is missing and who can credibly supply it. Distinguish a knowledge partner, data provider, implementation host, convening partner and resource partner. One organisation can play several roles, but interest in collaboration is not a commitment of funding, staff or data.

A value proposition connects a public-purpose problem, the partner’s legitimate interest, its specific contribution and a verifiable output. Avoid promising visibility, influence over scientific conclusions or commercial advantage in exchange for support. The Secretariat prepares and follows up opportunities; authorised FAO processes govern commitments and agreements.

From concept note to proposal

A concept note tests strategic fit: problem and evidence; target users; intended result; activities and outputs; partner roles; indicative resources; risks; and the next decision. A project proposal develops the agreed idea into a costed workplan, governance arrangements, monitoring plan, safeguards and formal responsibilities.

For donor engagement, first understand the resource partner’s priorities, eligibility and decision cycle. Present a concrete gap and options, record feedback, and revise the design without changing the scientific purpose merely to fit a funding preference. Keep pipeline stages explicit: identified; qualified; discussion; concept reviewed; proposal submitted; agreement signed. Only a confirmed agreement supports a funding claim.

Resources include technical time, data access, translation, training capacity and implementation sites as well as cash. Record whether contributions are proposed, confirmed or received; assess valuation and acceptance through the applicable procedures.

LEAP application: a feed-data and uptake pilot

Consider a hypothetical pilot supporting the revised feed guideline: a research institution supplies methodological expertise; a feed association helps identify representative cases; country institutions host application; a digital team builds a documented data template; and a resource partner supports review and training.

The pilot could organise new ingredient datasets, test practical examples concerning technologies or mitigation measures, consolidate expert comments and produce reviewed training materials. TAG or authorised technical experts retain judgement over methods, data quality and interpretation. The concept must not assume that a revised guideline or pilot has already been approved.

Digital innovation is useful when it removes a defined bottleneck—such as inconsistent metadata—not because an AI tool is fashionable. Specify provenance, versioning, access permissions, confidentiality and human technical review. A searchable tool is not an LCA model or an independent scientific validator.

Sustainable finance and responsible engagement

Sustainable finance can be an adjacent partnership theme: better environmental evidence may inform investment dialogue or transition support. It does not automatically establish investment eligibility, creditworthiness, financial additionality or a verified emissions reduction. Do not promise a bankable project from guideline training alone.

Before progressing private-sector engagement, use the current FAO screening, due-diligence and approval arrangements. Identify conflicts of interest, data ownership, publication restrictions and risks of endorsement or greenwashing. Industry data can improve relevance, while transparent assumptions and independent technical scrutiny protect credibility.

A sustainable partnership needs a post-pilot owner, recurring resources, maintenance responsibilities and an exit or handover route. A single sponsored workshop is not a durable delivery model.

Experience mapping and evidence boundary

Main case — ESCAP: Your partner coordination across 15 countries and 30+ organisations, feedback synthesis, concept materials, budget comparisons and donor briefs provide direct evidence of supporting programme development. Follow-up directions such as toolkit adaptation, trainer development and green-finance learning show how user demand can become structured workstreams. They do not prove that you independently raised funds, signed agreements or delivered all subsequent initiatives.

Private-sector comparison — Goertek: Customer communication, commercial documents and cross-functional resource coordination show how you connect an external need with internal delivery capacity. This is transferable to testing partner fit and feasible commitments. A commercial customer relationship is not equivalent to FAO partnership approval, donor negotiation or responsible-investment expertise.

Research/client comparison — LSE Student Consulting: Analysis of 5,000+ records and 10+ organisational benchmarks supports evidence-based needs diagnosis. For partnership development, it helps distinguish a demonstrated user problem from an attractive but unsupported concept. Recommendations alone do not establish client implementation or funding outcomes.

Gap: independent resource mobilisation, formal donor negotiation, private-sector due diligence and sustainable-finance structuring remain areas to develop. Your current strength is preparing the evidence, coordination and design inputs that allow authorised colleagues to progress an opportunity.

Five role-relevant expressions

  • qualify an opportunity against the work programme and partner capabilities
  • articulate a shared value proposition without compromising scientific independence
  • distinguish indicative contributions from confirmed commitments
  • develop a costed proposal from an agreed concept
  • design for continuity beyond the initial pilot

Private output · 5–10 minutes

Draft a one-page Partnership Opportunity Note for the hypothetical feed-data and uptake pilot. Use eight short fields: evidence-backed gap; target users/result; complementary partner roles; three tangible outputs; indicative cash/in-kind needs and confirmation status; governance/data safeguards; continuity plan; next decision and owner.

Add one digital-data opportunity and one carefully bounded sustainable-finance link. Label all partners as role types, not actual commitments. Leave costs “to estimate” rather than inventing a budget. End with the evidence needed before a donor conversation.

Keep the completed note private. Do not publish internal budgets, client information, donor correspondence or unreviewed exercise answers.
