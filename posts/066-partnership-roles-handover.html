Own the coordination, clarify the authority

Theme: governance and role clarity during a handover.

Objective

Explain how you kept a shared activity moving while distinguishing operational ownership from approval authority.

Core knowledge

A handover should clarify four roles: who coordinates, who performs each task, who approves consequential decisions, and who must be informed. These roles may belong to different people. Taking responsibility for coordination does not automatically give someone budget, procurement or technical approval authority.

For one deliverable, identify the owner, deadline, dependency and completion evidence. For an unresolved issue, identify the decision required and the appropriate approver. Share a concise status update rather than assuming silence means agreement.

English practice question

Tell me about a time you took over coordination of an activity and clarified responsibilities.

Keyword card

  • Partners: actual internal colleagues and external contributors.
  • Needs: what information or input did each require?
  • Bottleneck: one concrete unresolved handover issue.
  • Judgment: what could you coordinate, and what required approval?
  • Action: one clarification, one follow-up and one handoff you personally completed.
  • Evidence: an observable output or confirmed completion; leave unknown details blank.

Three natural phrases

  • take over the day-to-day coordination
  • clarify who was responsible for what
  • seek approval where needed

Follow-up

Which decision was yours to make, and which required someone else's approval?

Five-minute recording

One minute: choose an episode and note keywords. Ninety seconds: tell the story without a script. One minute: answer the follow-up. Ninety seconds: replay and improve only the distinction between personal action and approval authority.

Evidence boundary

Describe verified completion, not invented savings or crisis prevention. A successful event is a team output; explain your contribution separately. This public page contains a generic framework only. Personal experience cards, internal records and recordings remain private.
