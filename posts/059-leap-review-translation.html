Consolidating comments is not deciding the science

Technical Coordination · Knowledge Translation · Day 3 · 20 November 2026

JD tasks: coordinate experts and technical materials, organise review inputs, support knowledge products and communicate findings. Objective: build a traceable route from a reviewer’s comment to an authorised technical response, a revised passage and a usable explanation. Read for 5 minutes, then spend 5–10 minutes on the private matrix.

One review process, distinct responsibilities

Technical coordination means managing the interfaces around expert work, not replacing expert judgement. The Secretariat can classify comments, identify duplicates, flag contradictions, request evidence, track owners and dates, and check whether agreed edits appear in the correct version. Technical leads determine scientific acceptability under the applicable review arrangements.

Separate three functions: internal technical review checks method and coherence; peer review adds specialist scrutiny; public review gathers broader technical and practical feedback. These functions can overlap; do not assume a mandatory sequence or approval rule without the current terms of reference.

A review matrix needs closure evidence

The original LEAP feed-guideline public-review record provides a concrete precedent: comments are located by page, line and section; proposed changes and responses are recorded, including acceptance, rejection and clarification. Technical objections require supporting scientific or technical evidence.

A useful working matrix adds a unique comment ID, source version, category, technical response owner, proposed disposition, rationale, affected sections, revised version and closure check. “Response drafted” is not “edit verified.” Keep the original comment intact and link duplicates rather than silently deleting dissent.

Use precise statuses: received; clarification needed; assigned; response proposed; authorised response recorded; edit verified. A coordinator may verify that text changed, but should not mark a scientific objection resolved without the authorised response.

LEAP feed revision scenario

The current revision addresses new datasets, ingredients, technologies, mitigation approaches and cases, with links to other LEAP environmental guidance. Imagine two reviewers disagreeing about a regional by-product dataset: one prioritises local relevance; another questions missing processing-energy data.

Do not average their opinions or choose the easier edit. Record the competing concerns, ask the technical lead what evidence is needed and route the question to the relevant experts. Track consequential edits to tables, figures, worked examples and training slides. A change in an assumption may affect several products, not only one paragraph.

This is a hypothetical coordination exercise, not an actual TAG decision. The announced programme includes technical, peer and public review and later dissemination/capacity development; it does not prove that any particular meeting or review has already occurred.

Harmonisation is not identical local inputs

Harmonisation protects a common methodological basis so results can be interpreted consistently. Local application may require region-specific datasets, ingredients, energy systems or examples, but substitutions must be transparent and consistent with the applicable guidance.

Knowledge translation should retain the question, system boundary, functional unit, allocation assumptions, data limitations and uncertainty relevant to the claim. An accessible brief can omit equations that the audience does not need; it must not omit a limitation that changes the meaning of the result.

A translation note should distinguish what the global guidance specifies, what the local user needs, what can be adapted, what requires expert confirmation and what cannot yet be concluded. Keep separate technical clearance and editorial/plain-language checks.

Experience connections: evidence to usable knowledge

Main case — LSE Student Consulting: Your analysis of 5,000+ user records, Python work, benchmarking of 10+ organisations and visualised client report demonstrate a chain from source evidence to recommendations. For LEAP, the transferable discipline is to keep an accessible conclusion linked to the analysis behind it. It is not evidence of LCA validation or feed-sector technical review.

Supporting case — Intellisia Institute: AI-governance research, white-paper/policy-report contributions and institutional content illustrate translation between specialist debates and policy-facing audiences. The lesson is to preserve definitions, sources and contested points while changing structure and language. Do not claim independent authorship of every institutional output or technical authority over the field.

Comparison — FAO Rwanda: Environmental background research and inputs to peatland/programme concepts show experience connecting national priorities, stakeholder inputs and intervention logic. You supported research and drafting; you did not establish a national peatland definition or independently approve technical methods.

Gap: formal TAG review administration, LCA modelling and livestock/feed science remain learning needs. Your strongest current evidence concerns analytical synthesis, drafting, communication and follow-up around technical contributors.

Five role-relevant expressions

  • consolidate comments without prejudging the technical response
  • maintain traceability from the source comment to the verified edit
  • route methodological disagreements to the authorised technical lead
  • adapt the explanation without weakening the evidence boundary
  • distinguish editorial clarity from scientific clearance

Private output · 5–10 minutes

Create a Technical Review and Translation Matrix with three hypothetical rows: missing energy data; conflicting allocation suggestions; an overconfident claim in a training slide. Columns: comment ID/source version; issue and supporting evidence; technical owner; proposed response/status; affected outputs; revised version/closure check; plain-language message and limitation.

Leave technical decisions “pending expert review” where you lack evidence. Add a two-sentence audience note explaining one issue to a programme user without inventing a numerical result. This is a reusable coordination tool, not a standard interview answer.

Keep your completed matrix private. Use no internal project files, reviewer identities or unpublished comments.
