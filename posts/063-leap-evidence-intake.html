NO.63 · CAPABILITY DEEPENING

New data are not automatically usable evidence

2026.11.24

Capabilities, JD mapping and objective

Technical Coordination · Knowledge Management & Evidence, supported by Knowledge Translation. 24 November 2026 · 10–15 minutes.

JD tasks: coordinate expert submissions and technical materials, maintain reliable records and support knowledge products. Objective: distinguish an administratively complete submission from evidence that experts consider fit for a particular use. This deepens the review workflow rather than repeating the introductory matrix.

Intake is a gate, not scientific approval

When a contributor sends a dataset, the Secretariat can check whether files open, required metadata are present, permissions are stated, versions are identifiable and the intended use is explicit. It cannot infer scientific validity from a complete form or a prestigious contributor.

Use separate statuses: received; metadata clarification needed; ready for expert review; technical disposition recorded; authorised use documented. “Ready for review” must never become “validated” by accident. Record who made each judgement and which version it applies to.

Five questions before routing a submission

  • Provenance: who collected or compiled it, by what method, and where is the original source?
  • Representativeness: which geography, period, technology and production conditions does it cover? A local case is not automatically a global default.
  • Comparability: what units, reference basis, boundaries and allocation assumptions are documented? Experts must judge whether conversion or reconciliation is defensible.
  • Completeness and uncertainty: which flows are absent, estimated or measured? Missing values are not zero; a proxy is not a measurement.
  • Rights and review: may data be shared, published or reused? What technical checks have occurred, and what is still pending?

These are proposed coordination prompts, not official LEAP acceptance criteria or a substitute for expert assessment.

LEAP scenario: a new circular feed ingredient

The announced feed-guideline revision includes new datasets, ingredients, technologies, mitigation approaches and regional cases, alongside data-quality and uncertainty work. Imagine a contributor submitting processing-energy data for a food-industry by-product.

The file may state kWh per tonne without explaining wet versus dry mass, the processing stages included or the reference year. The coordinator requests missing metadata rather than choosing a conversion or an allocation method. The relevant expert then determines whether the evidence can support a worked example, needs sensitivity analysis or is unsuitable for the proposed use.

Preserve the raw submission, give the clarified version a new identifier and link the technical response to any chapter, case or training material that uses it. A public summary should describe the permitted use and limitations without exposing confidential data. This is hypothetical, not an actual TAG decision.

Experience connections and evidence boundaries

Main case — LSE Student Consulting: Your work with 5,000+ user records, Python analysis and 10+ organisational benchmarks provides direct experience moving from source material to analysis and client recommendations. The transferable question is whether different records or benchmarks genuinely support the same comparison. Use the methods you actually performed; do not invent a particular cleaning procedure, validation test or data defect.

Comparison — Jilin University forest-governance research: Interviews, focus groups, questionnaires and NVivo work connect different forms of evidence. A government interview, community response and reserve document may address different questions and carry different limitations. Triangulation does not mean treating all sources as interchangeable or claiming that a sample represents every community.

Boundary: these experiences support source organisation, analytical judgement and evidence synthesis. They do not establish LCA modelling, environmental-inventory validation or feed-science authority. Technical acceptance and generalisation remain expert responsibilities.

Five useful expressions

  • check submission completeness without implying scientific validation
  • document provenance and the intended use of the evidence
  • flag incompatible units and undocumented assumptions
  • distinguish measured values, estimates and missing data
  • link the authorised use to a specific version and review record

Private output · 5–10 minutes

Build a one-page Evidence Intake Card for one hypothetical ingredient dataset. Fields: evidence ID/version; intended use; original source/method; geography/time/technology; unit/reference basis; boundary/allocation notes; missing or estimated fields; uncertainty information; reuse permission; clarification request; expert owner; review status.

Mark unknown fields “not provided.” Write two clarification requests and one routing note. Do not decide whether the dataset is scientifically acceptable. Add a one-line public summary that preserves the main limitation. Keep your completed card private.
