A tracker is not a workplan: building a Secretariat delivery control system

10–15 minutes · Workplan / Actions / Decisions / Dependencies / Evidence

2026.11.17

01｜Capability and JD connection

Capability 1Programme Delivery

Capability 8Knowledge Management & Evidence

JD connectionFollow up agreed actions, workplans, timelines, deliverables and Secretariat records.

Today’s objective is to distinguish a list of tasks from a delivery system. By the end, you should be able to design one view that tells a Secretariat manager what is due, what is blocked, which decision is missing and where the supporting evidence sits.

A useful tracker does not merely record activity. It makes the next management decision visible.

02｜Why a task list is not enough

A feed-guideline revision may involve TAG experts, chapter leads, reviewers, FAO technical officers, editors and dissemination colleagues. “Review Chapter 4” is therefore incomplete. Delivery depends on the owner, the expected output, the evidence required, the upstream dependency, the approving authority and the consequence of delay.

WorkplanDefines outputs, work packages, milestones, owners and target dates.

Action logRecords the next concrete commitment arising from a meeting or review.

Decision logRecords what was agreed, by whom, on what basis and what the decision changes.

Risk logIdentifies uncertain events, their effect, owner, mitigation and escalation point.

Evidence indexLinks the claim or deliverable to the current source, version and review status.

These records should connect. If a TAG decision changes the recommended allocation approach, the decision log should point to the affected chapter, the action log should identify the revision owner and deadline, and the evidence index should show the source used.

03｜A minimum viable Secretariat dashboard

Use one row per deliverable or decision-dependent action, with the following fields:

  • Workstream and deliverable: the output being produced, not a vague activity
  • Owner and contributors: one accountable owner plus named inputs
  • Due date and status: on track, at risk, blocked or complete
  • Dependency: the input or decision required before progress can continue
  • Next action: one observable step, with a person and date
  • Decision required: the authority and deadline for the decision
  • Evidence/version link: the current file, source or review record
  • Risk and escalation: consequence, mitigation and escalation trigger

04｜LEAP application scenario

Suppose the animal-feed TAG is revising a section on a new feed ingredient. One expert has submitted new evidence, another questions its geographic representativeness, and the chapter lead cannot finalize the example until the data-quality requirement is agreed.

A weak tracker says: “Update case study — ongoing.” A useful Secretariat dashboard says: “Case-study revision — at risk; chapter lead owns final text; dependency is TAG agreement on geographic representativeness; Secretariat circulates a two-option decision note by 20 November; technical lead confirms the option by 24 November; current evidence and comments are linked in the review matrix.”

The Secretariat has not made the scientific decision. It has made the decision pathway, ownership and documentary trail manageable.

05｜Connection to your CV: more than two UN examples

Main case — Goertek: You currently coordinate six projects across mass production, new product introduction and bidding. You consolidate customer requirements and inputs from R&D, quality and production, track milestones and outstanding actions, and have identified and communicated project risks on two occasions. This is direct evidence of delivery control in a fast-moving environment: multiple workstreams, interdependent teams, commercial deliverables and timely escalation.

Supporting case — LSE Student Consulting: You analysed more than 5,000 user records and benchmarked over ten peer organizations before translating the evidence into visualisations, a client report and recommendations. This demonstrates a different part of Secretariat discipline: maintaining an evidence chain from raw information to an accessible decision product.

Additional transfer: ESCAP adds partner workplans, budgets, participant records and donor reporting; FAO Rwanda adds implementation monitoring, procurement records and final reporting. These are useful supporting examples, but today’s strongest comparison is between Goertek’s delivery control and LSE Consulting’s evidence control.

06｜Evidence boundary

You can credibly claim experience tracking multiple projects, actions, risks, evidence and reporting inputs. You should not claim that you have administered a LEAP TAG, owned a formal FAO Secretariat decision register or controlled the technical approval of an LCA guideline. The transferable capability is building reliable coordination infrastructure around specialists and decision-makers.

07｜Five role-relevant expressions

  • translate the work programme into owned and time-bound deliverables
  • surface dependencies before they become delivery delays
  • maintain a traceable record of decisions and supporting evidence
  • escalate material risks with a proposed next action
  • distinguish technical ownership from coordination accountability
5–10 MIN OUTPUT

Build a one-page LEAP Secretariat Delivery Dashboard

Create four hypothetical rows: TAG evidence review, revised chapter submission, public-review response matrix and uptake webinar package. Use these columns: deliverable; accountable owner; due date; status; dependency; next action; decision required; evidence/version; risk and escalation trigger.

Keep all entries hypothetical. Do not use unpublished FAO material, internal partner comments or private project information.

Today’s takeaway

Programme delivery and knowledge management are one system: the workplan shows what should happen, while the action, decision, risk and evidence records explain whether—and on what basis—it can happen.
