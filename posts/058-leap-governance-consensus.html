Partnership Governance · Consultation & Facilitation

JD connection: prepare and follow up Steering Committee, TAG and Scientific Advisory Group meetings; support partners, consultations and the work programme. Today: separate representation, scientific advice, approval and process coordination.

10–15 minutes: 5 minutes reading plus 5–10 minutes producing a private governance map and consensus log.

Governance before facilitation

A stakeholder map shows who matters. A governance map adds who can advise, decide, approve, implement and challenge. Participation does not automatically confer approval authority; technical expertise does not automatically confer authority over the partnership work programme.

  • Steering Committee: overall leadership and approval of the LEAP work programme, bringing government, private-sector and civil-society perspectives together.
  • TAG: multidisciplinary technical work on assessment guidelines and methods; technical judgement must remain with the authorised experts.
  • Scientific Advisory Group: explicitly named in this JD. Verify its current terms of reference and relationship with other bodies before assigning a review or approval role; the public governance overview does not establish a complete decision-rights chart for it.
  • Secretariat: organise the process, clarify requests, circulate evidence, record outcomes and follow actions. Do not substitute your own technical judgement for the experts’ decision.

Before a meeting, confirm its mandate, chair, expected outcome and decision procedure. Do not invent quorum, voting or approval rules from a general organisational chart.

Design consultation around the decision

Start with a bounded question, not a broad invitation to “share views.” Identify whose interests are affected, whose knowledge is needed and whose voice may otherwise be absent. Ask for evidence and proposed changes in advance; distribute a short decision brief with options and unresolved questions.

During discussion, separate factual uncertainty, methodological disagreement and competing interests. Give less powerful participants a structured opportunity to contribute. Summarise each position neutrally and ask participants to correct the record. Silence is not agreement.

Close with explicit outcomes: agreed; agreed subject to a condition; unresolved pending evidence; or referred to the appropriate body. Record any material dissent and its rationale, alongside an owner and deadline for the next step. Circulate the draft record for factual correction without presenting it as approved before the authorised process is complete.

LEAP application: a circular feed ingredient

The current feed-guideline revision includes new data, ingredients, technologies, mitigation measures, regional cases and review. Consider a hypothetical consultation on a food-industry by-product. Industry may emphasise feasibility and confidentiality; researchers may question allocation and data quality; government users may seek comparability; civil society may raise environmental trade-offs.

The Secretariat can group comments by issue, link supporting material and distinguish requests for clarification from technical objections. The TAG addresses methodological questions; the Secretariat checks which body owns any scope or work-programme change. A follow-up webinar should explain both the resulting guidance and its limitations, not announce consensus where disagreement remains.

This is a learning scenario, not a report of an actual TAG decision or an official prescribed approval sequence.

Experience connections and evidence boundary

Main case — Jilin University forest-governance research: Your work connected communities, government agencies and a nature reserve through interviews, focus groups, questionnaires and qualitative analysis. The governance lesson is to distinguish a community’s lived experience, an agency’s statutory responsibility and a research team’s analytical role. Different evidence sources can reveal different interests without any one interviewee representing an entire constituency. Your co-drafted ecological-compensation policy brief was adopted by the relevant Jilin CPPCC committee; this supports evidence-to-policy contribution, not a claim that you personally decided policy or verified long-term community outcomes.

Comparison — Intellisia Institute: Forum partner and guest coordination, stakeholder research and policy-content preparation demonstrate convening and preparation across perspectives. Speaker participation is evidence of engagement, not proof of a negotiated agreement or formal governance mandate.

Supporting comparison — ESCAP: Coordination across 15 countries and 30+ organisations adds experience with agendas, institutional priorities, consolidated inputs and follow-up. It does not establish that you chaired a formal Steering Committee or held decision authority over partner institutions.

Gap: formal LEAP governance administration, technical consensus rules and scientific advisory procedures remain learning needs. Your direct strengths are stakeholder research, communication, evidence synthesis and operational follow-through.

Five useful expressions

  • clarify the mandate and decision rights of each partnership body
  • distinguish consultation input from formal approval
  • surface differing interests without prejudging the technical outcome
  • record qualified agreement and unresolved objections accurately
  • close the consultation loop with an owner, deadline and response

Private output · 5–10 minutes

Create a one-page Stakeholder–Governance Map + Consensus Log for the hypothetical feed-ingredient consultation.

Map five roles: Steering Committee, TAG, Secretariat, data provider and intended guideline user. Columns: interest; contribution; decision/advisory role; mandate to verify; engagement method.

Add three blank log rows using: issue; positions and evidence; agreement status; unresolved point; decision owner; next action/date; record/version. Mark uncertain authority “to verify,” not “approved.” Include a 20-minute agenda: purpose and mandate (3), evidence and options (7), structured perspectives (6), outcomes and actions (4).

Do not publish your completed answer or use internal comments, personal stakeholder details or confidential employer material.
