Scope before data: aligning system boundary and functional unit for the feed guidelines revision

10–15 minutes · System boundary / Functional unit / Reference flow / Review coordination

2026.11.16

01｜Today’s objective

Understand why a Technical Advisory Group must align the study goal, system boundary and comparison basis before discussing new datasets, feed ingredients or mitigation options. Learn what a Secretariat coordination professional needs to capture, clarify and follow up—without pretending to make the technical decision.

A technically rich dataset cannot repair a comparison whose scope and reference basis were never made explicit.

02｜The live LEAP context

FAO LEAP launched the revision of the Environmental Performance of Animal Feeds Supply Chains guidelines in October 2026. The 2016 guidance provides a harmonized life-cycle framework covering feed production, processing, compound-feed manufacture, on-farm ration preparation, transport and trade. The revision is expected to run for approximately twelve months, from November 2026 to October 2027.

The TAG is expected to review methods, scientific references, figures, default data, allocation approaches, data-quality requirements, uncertainty and reporting; assess emerging ingredients, innovations and mitigation options; develop cases; contribute to technical, peer and public review; and support dissemination and capacity development. This makes scope alignment both a technical requirement and a coordination requirement.

03｜Four concepts that must not be mixed

GoalWhy the assessment is being conducted, who will use it and what decision or comparison it is intended to support.

System boundaryWhich life-cycle stages, processes, geography and time period are included or excluded. In the feed guidance, modules cover feed production, processing, compound-feed production and the farm stage, with transport and storage connected between stages.

Functional unitThe quantified function used as the basis for comparison. It tells reviewers what the results are “per” and whether two options genuinely provide a comparable function.

Reference flowThe amount of product needed to fulfil the defined function. The 2016 feed guidance uses weight-based reference units and asks that dry-matter content and gross energy be recorded as metadata.

04｜Why the boundary changes the answer

New ingredientIf an alternative feed uses a food-industry co-product, where does the burden of the upstream process sit?

New technologyIf processing lowers feed losses but uses more energy, are production, processing and transport all inside the comparison?

Mitigation claimIf a measure reduces emissions per kilogram of feed but changes nutritional value, is the comparison functionally equivalent?

These are not questions the Partnership Specialist should answer alone. The coordination task is to ensure that the relevant experts see the same question, use the same decision record and state the evidence supporting their recommendation.

05｜Allocation, data quality and uncertainty enter next

Allocation addresses how environmental burdens are divided when a process produces more than one useful output. Data quality asks whether evidence is sufficiently representative in time, geography, technology and completeness. Uncertainty makes visible how sensitive results are to data limitations, modelling choices and assumptions.

The sequence matters: first agree the goal and scope; then identify processes and data needs; then examine allocation choices, data quality and uncertainty. Otherwise experts may submit technically sound evidence that answers different questions.

06｜What Secretariat coordination looks like in practice

BeforeCirculate a scope note listing the proposed goal, modules, comparison basis, exclusions, open questions and required evidence.

DuringRecord decisions separately from unresolved issues; attribute technical recommendations to the relevant expert or evidence source.

AfterMaintain an action tracker with owner, due date, dependency and document location; follow up on missing evidence and revised text.

ReviewUse a comment matrix linking each proposal to section, rationale, supporting source, disposition and TAG response.

This role enables technical quality through process discipline. It does not replace the judgement of LCA, feed or livestock specialists.

07｜Connection to your experience—and the evidence boundary

Direct evidence: At ESCAP, you coordinated inputs from governments, business-support organizations, experts and entrepreneurs across 15 countries and more than 30 organizations, prepared agendas and briefing materials, consolidated feedback and supported action follow-up. At FAO Rwanda, you coordinated technical trainers, participants, suppliers and internal teams for capacity-building activities and the Agriculture Show, while supporting programme records, procurement and reporting.

Transferable capability: You can structure a multi-party review process, distinguish decisions from action items, maintain version and submission discipline, and translate technical discussions into clear records and next steps.

Boundary: You have not conducted formal LCA modelling, selected allocation methods, assessed feed formulations or validated livestock environmental datasets. Your credible contribution is to facilitate expert work and make the evidence trail usable—not to claim technical authority you do not hold.

08｜Five role-relevant expressions

  • align the study goal, system boundary and comparison basis
  • capture both the technical rationale and the outstanding evidence gap
  • flag a potential mismatch in functional equivalence
  • consolidate expert comments into a traceable review matrix
  • follow up on revised text, supporting references and unresolved actions
5–10 MIN OUTPUT

Build a one-page Scope Alignment Note

For a hypothetical comparison between a conventional feed ingredient and an emerging circular feed ingredient, create six fields: assessment purpose; proposed functional unit; included life-cycle stages; three explicit exclusions; two open technical questions; and one evidence owner with a due date. End with one sentence stating which decision belongs to the TAG rather than the Secretariat.

Keep the output private. Use a hypothetical case and do not insert internal FAO, ESCAP or partner information.

Today’s takeaway

The Partnership Specialist does not need to decide the boundary alone. The job is to make the proposed boundary visible, bring the right experts to the same decision point, document the rationale and keep every follow-up traceable.
