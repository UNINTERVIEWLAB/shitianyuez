PARTNERSHIP PRACTICE · 26 NOVEMBER 2026 · 10 MINUTES

Different priorities, shared delivery

Theme: negotiation and coordination. A useful partnership example explains a decision, not just a list of stakeholders.

Today's objective

Explain how you identified a dependency, understood competing priorities and secured a specific next step without claiming authority you did not have.

Core framework

Separate a partner's position (what they request) from their underlying need (why it matters). Clarify the deadline, dependency and consequence before proposing an option. Agreement should end with an owner, due date and observable completion criterion. Escalation is a request for a decision, not evidence that you personally resolved everything.

Public practice card

  • Partners: who depended on whom?
  • Needs: what did each party need to protect?
  • Bottleneck: what could not move forward?
  • Judgment: what evidence determined your next step?
  • Action: what did you personally clarify, propose or follow up?
  • Result: what changed, and what record supports it?

Three useful phrases

  • clarify competing priorities
  • agree on a workable next step
  • make the dependency visible

Five-minute recording exercise

Spend one minute choosing a real coordination episode and noting six keywords. Speak for 60–90 seconds without a script. Then answer: “Why did you choose that approach rather than escalate immediately?” Record a shorter second attempt. Review only one thing: whether your personal decision and its basis were clear.

Evidence boundary

Coordinating a response is not the same as approving a technical solution, controlling another team's resources or demonstrating long-term partnership impact. Use a documented output or an observable change; leave an unverified result blank. Personal cards, recordings, client information and internal commercial details are not published here.
