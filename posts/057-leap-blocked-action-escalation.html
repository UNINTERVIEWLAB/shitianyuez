NO. 57 · SIX-DAY SPRINT · DAY 1

A blocked action needs a decision, not another reminder

10–15 minutes · Programme Delivery / Knowledge Management & Evidence

2026.11.18

01｜Capability tags and JD task map

Capability 1Programme Delivery

Capability 8Knowledge Management & Evidence

JD tasksMaintain the work programme; follow actions and deliverables; coordinate expert inputs; document decisions, risks and evidence; support reporting.

Today’s objective: diagnose why an agreed action is blocked and convert it into the right management response. By the end, you should be able to distinguish a reminder from an escalation package and show how the outcome updates the workplan, decision log and evidence trail.

Escalation is not transferring ownership upward. It is bringing the right decision to the person who has the authority to make it.

02｜From a tracker to a delivery-control cycle

A tracker records what is open. A Secretariat control cycle explains what prevents closure and what must happen next:

1 · DeliverableDefine the observable output and its milestone—not “work with experts,” but “consolidated TAG review matrix circulated.”

2 · DependencyName the data, review, approval or upstream action required before work can continue.

3 · ActionAssign one person, one concrete next step and one date.

4 · Blocker diagnosisIdentify whether the problem is missing information, limited capacity, competing priority or absent decision authority.

5 · Decision / escalationState the choice required, the decision owner and the trigger for escalation.

6 · Evidence and forecastLink the current source/version, record the decision and revise the delivery forecast.

The management value lies in the links. A delayed dataset changes the review timetable; a technical disagreement creates a decision requirement; a decision changes chapter actions; each change must be traceable in the status report.

03｜Four blockers require four different responses

Information gapThe contributor does not know the requested format, scope or evidence standard. Response: clarify the specification and provide a template or example.

Capacity constraintThe owner agrees but lacks time, data access or analytical support. Response: reduce scope, sequence the work, add support or revise the date.

Priority conflictTwo legitimate commitments compete. Response: make the delivery impact visible and request management alignment on priority.

Authority gapThe person cannot approve the method, disclosure or resource commitment. Response: route a bounded decision to the correct technical lead, TAG co-lead or Secretariat manager.

Repeated reminders only help when the task is understood, feasible, prioritised and within the recipient’s authority. Otherwise, they add communication without removing the constraint.

04｜LEAP application: feed-guideline revision

The animal-feed guideline revision is expected to run from November 2026 to October 2027. Its TAG may review methodology, references, default data, new ingredients, technologies, mitigation options, case studies and draft materials. Technical, peer and public review, dissemination and capacity development create a chain of dependent outputs.

Imagine that a chapter lead is waiting for regional evidence on a new circular feed ingredient. The Secretariat should not immediately label the expert “late.” It should diagnose the blockage:

  • Information: Is the data-quality requirement or case-study template unclear?
  • Capacity: Does the expert need a narrower request or support from another contributor?
  • Priority: Is the voluntary expert balancing this review against other commitments?
  • Authority: Does the TAG need to decide whether proxy data are acceptable?

The Secretariat coordinates the process; it does not decide the technical acceptability of proxy data on behalf of the TAG. Its job is to present the evidence gap, schedule impact and feasible options to the authorised technical decision-maker, then document the outcome and update affected actions.

05｜What an escalation package should contain

Requested outputExact deliverable, owner and due date

EvidenceRequests made, responses, current version and unresolved gap

ImpactAffected milestone, reviewer or downstream product

OptionsTwo or three feasible responses with trade-offs

Decision requiredWhat must be decided, by whom and by when

After the decisionUpdate action, decision, risk and evidence records

A useful status line is therefore not “Expert input pending.” It is: “Regional dataset v2 is pending; without it, Chapter 3 review slips five working days. The chapter lead must decide by 24 November whether to accept proxy data or narrow the case study.”

06｜Experience mapping: direct evidence, transfer and limits

Primary case — Goertek Business Project Manager: You coordinate six projects across mass production, NPI and bidding; consolidate customer requirements and cross-functional inputs; track milestones and outstanding actions; and have warned customers of project risks on two occasions. Your current difficulty—an item may move when your manager requests it but not when you do—shows a real programme-delivery problem: responsibility without equal formal authority. The credible learning is that you are beginning to separate execution problems from priority and authority problems, and to escalate with actions taken, delivery impact and a proposed decision.

Supporting comparison — UN ESCAP: Across a programme involving 15 countries and more than 30 organisations, you coordinated participant and partner inputs, agendas, materials, budgets, feedback and follow-up. Clear requests, deadlines and consolidated records helped a distributed group contribute to one programme. Unlike an internal corporate hierarchy, this relied primarily on shared purpose, process visibility and relationship management.

Evidence-control comparison — LSE Student Consulting: The chain from 5,000+ user records and ten-plus organisational benchmarks to analysis, visualisation and client recommendations shows why a delivery decision should be supported by a traceable evidence base rather than memory or assertion.

Additional supporting evidence: FAO Rwanda adds procurement documentation, implementation monitoring, MEL and final reporting. These show disciplined records across activity delivery, but not formal ownership of an organisation-wide decision register.

07｜Evidence boundary

You can claim experience coordinating interdependent work, tracking milestones and actions, managing records, identifying risks and escalating delivery concerns. You should not claim that the Goertek coordination challenge has already been fully resolved, that you formally governed a LEAP TAG, or that you made technical decisions on LCA, feed science or acceptable datasets. The transferable strength is your ability to make ownership, blockage, impact and the required decision visible.

08｜Five role-relevant expressions

  • distinguish an execution blockage from an authority-level decision
  • escalate with evidence, delivery impact and a proposed way forward
  • make dependencies and decision rights visible
  • retain coordination ownership after management alignment
  • update the workplan and decision trail after the issue is resolved
5–10 MIN PRIVATE OUTPUT

Build a Blocked-Action Escalation Panel

Create three hypothetical LEAP rows: new ingredient dataset, allocation-method review and regional case-study submission. Use these fields: deliverable; owner; due date; blocker type; evidence of follow-up; milestone impact; two response options; decision owner; escalation trigger; next action; evidence/version link.

For each row, write one sentence explaining why another reminder would—or would not—solve the problem. Keep the exercise private and use no unpublished FAO, employer or partner information.

Today’s takeaway

Influence without formal authority does not mean avoiding escalation. It means using clear requests, traceable evidence and agreed triggers—then escalating only the decision that the working level cannot make.
