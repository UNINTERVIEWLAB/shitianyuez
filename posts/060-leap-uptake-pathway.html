A completed course is not an adopted guideline

Uptake & Capacity Development · 10–15 minutes

2026.11.21

Capability and JD task map

Uptake & Capacity Development · Sprint Day 4 · 21 November 2026

JD connection: coordinate training, technical guidance, dissemination and knowledge sharing, while supporting guideline application and feedback. Objective: design support around a user’s real task and match each adoption claim to observable evidence. Read for 5 minutes; complete the private pathway in 5–10 minutes.

Five stages, five different claims

This practical pathway is a learning framework, not an official LEAP classification. Users may move backwards or need repeated support.

  • Dissemination: the intended user can find or receive the right version. Evidence: targeted distribution, verified access or download. A download is not proof of reading.
  • Understanding: the user can explain or perform a defined task. Evidence: an assessed worked exercise, not satisfaction alone.
  • Trial: the user applies a selected method to a bounded case with support. Evidence: a dated draft inventory or case note, reviewed against stated criteria.
  • Adoption: the method is actually used in the user’s work. Evidence: a completed assessment with documented assumptions and expert quality checks. Self-reports need clear labelling.
  • Institutionalization: repeated use is supported by roles, procedures, resources and quality assurance. Evidence: an approved SOP, assigned staff and repeated completed work—not a single application.

Design the learning around the task

Ask what the user needs to do, what prevents it and what support is appropriate. Awareness gaps may need outreach; conceptual gaps need examples; application gaps need technical clinics; missing data or organisational authority require more than another webinar.

For guideline users, combine a short orientation or e-learning module, a worked case, a supported application and follow-up technical guidance. Trainers and experts should verify the exercise and its marking criteria. The Secretariat coordinates audience needs, materials, schedules and feedback; it does not certify LCA competence without an authorised assessment process.

A trainer-of-trainers approach needs checks on facilitation and technical accuracy, updated materials and support for subsequent sessions. Training trainers does not automatically prove successful cascade delivery.

LEAP feed-revision application

Imagine a capacity-development package for a revised feed-supply-chain guideline: users need to assess a new ingredient and understand updated datasets or mitigation measures. First identify audience roles—data providers, assessment practitioners, policy users and trainers have different tasks.

A proposed learning sequence could cover version changes; a scoped case with boundary and functional-unit choices; a dataset-quality exercise; discussion of allocation and uncertainty; and a supported application clinic. The TAG or authorised technical experts validate methodological content, including links to water, biodiversity, nutrient flows or circularity where relevant.

Collect recurring questions and failed application steps in a feedback log. Route methodological questions to experts, improve examples when wording is unclear and update the version register when guidance changes. Do not let local simplification silently remove assumptions or uncertainty.

This is a hypothetical design for learning, not evidence that the revised guideline or a specific training package has already been released.

Indicators that do not overclaim

Define the unit, denominator, period and evidence threshold before counting. For example: eligible pilot organisations, not a mix of organisations and individual webinar attendees.

  • Understanding: participants meeting a pre-defined exercise criterion / participants assessed; also report assessed / enrolled to expose attrition.
  • Trial: eligible pilot organisations submitting a reviewable case / all eligible pilot organisations, by the agreed date.
  • Adoption: pilot organisations with verified use meeting stated criteria / the original eligible pilot cohort. Report non-response and unresolved technical checks separately.
  • Institutionalization: organisations with documented procedures, responsibility and repeated verified use / eligible organisations followed over the specified period.

Record baseline practice so existing users are not counted as new adopters. An increase in use does not by itself prove that training caused it; environmental improvement requires a separate assessment. Set targets only after assessing baseline, resources and feasibility.

Experience connections and evidence boundaries

Main case — FAO Rwanda: You coordinated two rounds of hydroponic and vertical-farming training for approximately 20 young participants, supporting technical resources, participant communication and delivery. The case demonstrates translating a practical learning need into organised training and linking specialists with participants. It also illustrates why attendance, demonstrated practice and continued use should be recorded separately. Do not infer sustained adoption, yield gains or environmental benefits without follow-up evidence.

Comparison — ESCAP: Toolkit-related exchanges and feedback across countries show how learning can connect to country adaptation and follow-up needs. Participant-reported understanding or tool use is useful evidence when the respondent group, question and observation period are stated. It is not independent verification of institutional adoption or policy impact.

Contrasting evidence — Jilin University: Adoption of the co-drafted ecological-compensation brief by the relevant committee is an institutional policy-input outcome. It is different from individual learning or repeated operational application. It does not establish that all recommendations were implemented or that long-term community outcomes resulted.

Capability gap: you have relevant training coordination and evidence-synthesis experience, not formal LEAP training certification, livestock/feed expertise or verified responsibility for long-term guideline uptake. The next skill is designing proportionate follow-up and engaging technical reviewers.

Five useful expressions

  • design capacity development around a defined application task
  • distinguish dissemination reach from verified uptake
  • pair self-reported use with proportionate supporting evidence
  • close the feedback loop between users and technical experts
  • embed repeated application in organisational procedures

Private output · 5–10 minutes

Create a one-page LEAP Guideline Uptake Pathway. Use five rows: dissemination; understanding; trial; adoption; institutionalization. Columns: target user/task; main barrier; support format; responsible role; indicator with numerator/denominator and period; evidence source; feedback action.

Choose one hypothetical feed-assessment pilot cohort and keep the unit consistent. Add a baseline check and a non-response note. Leave technical acceptance criteria “to be confirmed with experts” where needed. This is a design exercise, not a completed answer or an interview script.

Keep your completed output private. Use no real participant records, unpublished employer material or unsupported results.
